PREDICTIONS WRITTEN BEFORE MEASUREMENT (Exp880 discipline, first used Exp871)

HYPOTHESIS: Exp849/Exp879 tell the product to "raise -c for longer sessions" and assert those levers
"do not affect the metric". That claim was never MEASURED - Exp849 only ever ran SMALLER -c, on runs
that DIED. Test what a larger n_ctx costs at FIXED audio, and whether its memory cost is RESIDENT
(touched at init) or VIRTUAL (reserved only). The two answers imply opposite product advice.

MECHANISM (from the cost model, Exp870/871, in my own units):
  decode = 95.8 ms/token + 13.22 us x P_used, prefill = 33.9 ms/row + 5.0 us x P_used.
  P_used depends on the audio and the tokens emitted, NOT on n_ctx. So n_ctx appears in NO term of the
  model => at fixed audio, rtf must be flat. n_ctx is a RESERVATION; the model prices USE.

PREDICTIONS (protocol clip stream_10s_24k.wav, 39 tokens, P_used ~ 67 positions):
  P1  rtf(-c 8192)  == rtf(-c 4096) within +-0.3 %   (protocol noise floor sd 0.233 %/rep, Exp867h)
  P2  rtf(-c 16384) == rtf(-c 4096) within +-0.3 %
      FAIL mode: a fixed cost proportional to n_ctx (zeroing/mapping the KV buffer, worse TLB or slot
      bookkeeping) would show as a monotone rtf rise with n_ctx. That is the real question.
  P3  KV bytes per position = 28.0 KB (28 layers x 2 x 2 kv_heads x 128 dim x F16; the code comment
      says exactly this and Exp849 used it for its +338 MB claim). Two admissible outcomes, and which
      one happens decides the product advice:
        P3a TOUCHED at init  -> RSS(+8192) = +112 MB, RSS(+16384) = +345 MB   (resident cost, real)
        P3b RESERVE only     -> RSS(+8192) ~= RSS(+16384) ~= RSS(4096) +- 5 MB (virtual only: raising
                               -c is cheaper than the docs claim, and the "+338 MB" note is wrong)
      Whichever it is, the DECISION-RELEVANT statement is RSS, not the 28 KB/position arithmetic.
  P4  load_s may rise with a bigger reservation (it is EXCLUDED from rtf by construction) - if it does,
      that is where the cost of n_ctx lands, not in the metric.
  P5  Plumbing control: ARGS='-c 1048576' must FAIL, not run (28 GB KV on an 8 GB phone). If it runs,
      either the flag never reached the binary (Exp865c class) or the allocation is lazy - and in the
      latter case P3b needs re-interpreting, so this arm is not optional.
  P6  Long-clip control (chat138, 1813 positions): rtf(-c 16384) == rtf(-c 4096) within +-0.5 %. At the
      long end the product claim lives; a fixed reservation penalty would be visible there too, and if
      only one clip can show the RSS law under a real session, it is this one.

AFTERWARD: if P1/P2/P6 hold, the honest product statement becomes "raise -c: costs 0 % of the metric
and <R> MB of RSS"; if P3b, "costs 0 % and no measurable RAM until the positions are used".

VERDICTS (measured after the predictions above):
  P1 HOLD: c8192 -0.8 % vs c4096 (3+3 interleaved reps, same 39 tokens).
  P2 HOLD: c16384 -1.8 % vs c4096. No fixed reservation cost; the sign is favourable.
  P3a TOUCHED AT INIT: RSS 2139.1 / 2303.6 / 2527.6 / 3820.2 MB at 4096/8192/16384/65536 =
  +27.4 KB/position linear. The "+338 MB" note was arithmetic; the measurement agrees with it.
  Product form: -c 8192 doubles the session for +112 MB committed at 0 % of the metric.
  P4 NOTED: load_s 0.8-1.2 s across arms, no n_ctx trend worth naming (excluded from rtf anyway).
  P5 CONFIRMED: -c 1048576 dies at context creation, silently (log ends at
  llama_new_context_with_model; no error, no summary, empty transcript). Runbook now has 3 modes.
  P6 AMENDED: fixed-order arms read +2.4 % at chat138; order-symmetric A,B,B,A reads +0.4 %.
  The first number was ordering bias, the second is the answer: -c is free at the long end too.
  Plumbing throughout: ARGS reaches the binary (device log shows n_ctx = 1048576 on the probe arm).
